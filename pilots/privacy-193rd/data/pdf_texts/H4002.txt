18 Section 1. As used in this chapter, the following words shall have the following 
19 meanings, unless the context clearly requires otherwise: 
20 “Authority” shall mean the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority established 
21 under chapter 161A. 
22 “Council” shall mean the office of transit safety council established under section 10. 
23 “Department” shall mean the Massachusetts Department of Transportation established 
24 under chapter 6C. 
25 “Executive director” shall mean the executive director of the office appointed in 
26 accordance with section 2. 
27 “Mass transportation services” shall mean public transportation operated by or pursuant 
28 to an agreement with the authority, department or a regional transit authority, including but not 
29 limited to public transportation provided by means of rail, including but not limited to subway 
30 and commuter rail, bus or other rubber-tired automotive vehicles, ferry or other vessels, or 
31 paratransit. 
32 “Office” shall mean the Office of Transit Safety established under section 2.

33 “Regional transit authority” shall mean a regional transit authority established under

35 Section 2. There is hereby established an Office of Transit Safety which shall be 
36 responsible for overseeing and securing the safe operation of mass transportation services and 
37 overseeing, investigating and enforcing compliance by the authority, department and regional 
38 transit authorities with applicable federal and state laws, regulations and policies related to transit 
39 safety. The office shall not be subject to the supervision or control of any board, bureau, 
40 department or other agency of the commonwealth except as specifically provided in this chapter. 
41 The office shall not operate public transportation services or receive any funds from the 
42 authority, department, a regional transit authority or other state, municipal or county agency or 
43 authority that operates public transportation services. 
44 Section 3. There shall be in the office an executive director, who shall be the 
45 administrative head of the agency. The executive director shall be appointed by a majority vote 
46 of the governor, attorney general, and secretary of state and shall be experienced in the field of 
47 transit operations or transit safety. 
48 The executive director shall serve for a term of three years, and no person shall be 
49 appointed for more than three three-year terms. In case of a vacancy in the position of executive 
50 director, a successor shall be appointed in the same manner for the unexpired term, and such 
51 successor shall thereafter be eligible to serve three three-year terms. 
52 The executive director may be removed from office for cause by a majority vote of the 
53 governor, attorney general, and secretary of state. Such cause shall include, but not be limited to, 
54 substantial neglect of duty, gross misconduct or conviction of a crime. The reasons for removal 
8 of 21 
55 of the executive director shall be stated in writing and shall include the basis for such removal. 
56 Such writing shall be sent to the clerk of the house of representatives and the clerk of the senate 
57 at the time of removal and shall be deemed to be a public document. 
58 The executive director may: (i) establish divisions and such other administrative units 
59 within the office and appoint such directors as they deem necessary; (ii) subject to appropriation, 
60 appoint and may remove such employees as they deem necessary to perform the duties of the 
61 office, including, but not limited to, counsels, paralegals, auditors, engineers, analysts and 
62 investigators; provided, that employees and contractors who are responsible for safety oversight, 
63 audits, or examinations of mass transportation services shall be qualified to perform such duties; 
64 and provided further, that employees and contractors who are directly responsible for safety 
65 oversight of or who conduct audits or examinations of rail fixed guideway public transportation 
66 or bus systems shall complete the public transportation safety certification training program in 
67 compliance with 49 CFR Part 672, as may be amended or replaced from time to time; and (iii) 
68 determine the salaries and duties of any employees; provided, that the total amount of all salaries 
69 shall not exceed the sum appropriated therefor by the general court. The provisions of sections 
70 nine A and forty-five of chapter thirty, chapter thirty-one and chapter one hundred and fifty E 
71 shall not apply to officers and employees within the office.

119 Section 5. The office shall be responsible for overseeing and securing the safe operation 
120 of mass transportation services and overseeing, investigating, and enforcing compliance by the 
121 authority, department and regional transit authorities with applicable federal and state laws, 
122 regulations and policies related to transit safety. The obligations and duties of the office shall 
123 include, but not be limited to: 
124 (a) upon being so certified by the Federal Transit Administration pursuant to 49 U.S.C. § 
125 5329, acting as the state safety oversight agency for the authority in compliance with all federal 
126 laws and regulations related thereto; 
127 (b) developing, adopting and periodically reviewing and revising regulations that set forth 
128 the duties and obligations of the office, authority, department, regional transit authorities and 
129 contractors with respect to the safety and safety oversight of mass transportation services; 
130 provided, that such regulations as applied to the authority shall meet the requirements of a state 
131 safety oversight program standard in compliance with 49 CFR Part 674, as may be amended or 
132 replaced from time to time; 
133 (c) approving, overseeing and enforcing compliance by the authority with the authority’s 
134 public transportation agency safety plan, as defined and regulated by 49 CFR Part 673, as may be 
135 amended or replaced from time to time; 
136 (d) conducting or overseeing investigations of and issuing or approving reports 
137 concerning hazards, safety events and near misses occurring on the premises of the authority, 
12 of 21 
138 department or a regional transit authority or occurring on or impacting any mass transportation 
139 service; 
140 (e) approving and overseeing compliance with corrective action plans submitted by the 
141 authority, department or a regional transit authority; 
142 (f) not later than 60 days following the issuance by a federal agency to a transit authority 
143 in another jurisdiction of a special directive or advisory related to transit safety, conducting an 
144 analysis of such special directive or advisory to determine whether the authority, department or 
145 regional transit authorities, as applicable, would be in compliance with the directive or advisory 
146 had it been issued to the authority, department or regional transit authorities; 
147 (g) retaining an auditor every 3 years to conduct an independent assessment of the 
148 authority’s compliance with federal and state safety laws, regulations and policies; 
149 (h) consulting with the authority, department, and regional transit authorities throughout 
150 the planning and development of capital investment plans and transit asset management plans, 
151 and any revisions thereto, for the purpose of assessing whether the authority and department are 
152 adequately prioritizing and programming capital expenditures that remediate identified 
153 unacceptable safety risks; and 
154 (i) implementing and publicizing a telephone number and email address that employees, 
155 contractors and members of the public may utilize for the purpose of notifying the office of 
156 hazards, safety events, and near misses affecting mass transportation services; provided, that any 
157 record of communications related thereto shall not be a public record under clause twenty-sixth 
158 of section 7 of chapter 4 or under chapter 66.

177 (e) compel compliance by the authority, department or a regional transit authority with
