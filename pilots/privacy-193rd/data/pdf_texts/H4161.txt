To the Honorable Senate and House of Representatives, 
 
 I am filing for your consideration a bill entitled, “An Act to Rename the Massachusetts 
Rehabilitation Commission, MassAbility, in order to Reflect the Self Determination and 
Limitless Possibilities of People with Disabilities.” 
 For many decades, people with disabilities have challenged mainstream culture to 
recognize disability not as the whole of who they are, but as one important aspect of their lives. 
This movement has inspired the Massachusetts Rehabilitation Commission (MRC) since its 
inception in 1956, shaping the values and programs of an agency committed to breaking down 
social barriers and expanding what is possible for people with disabilities across the 
Commonwealth. 
 Since 1956, MRC has provided vocational rehabilitation, job placement, independent 
living, and community-based services to empower and support people with disabilities to live life 
on their own terms, with equal rights, diversity, and accessibility as the priority. MRC currently 
serves approximately 20,000 individuals within the Commonwealth and is leading innovation 
through the NextGen program, a STEM focused rapid employment model for young adults with 
disabilities. 
 While expressing pride in MRC’s work and mission, members of the disability 
community have also long expressed concern with MRC’s name. The term “rehabilitation” has 
come to carry connotations that can feel stigmatizing or misleading. I believe that the name 
MassAbility is best suited not only to represent the values and mission of the agency, but to 
embody the self-determination, competence, and potential for success intrinsic to all people with 
disabilities. 

2 of 2 
 This legislation changes the name of the Massachusetts Rehabilitation Commission to 
MassAbility and removes uses of the term “handicap,” “handicapped,” and “retarded,” replacing 
them with “barrier,” “person with a disability,” and “person with intellectual disabilities.” The 
legislation further repeals G.L. c. 6 § 78A, providing for the administration of an antiquated, and 
now non-existent, program for extended sheltered employment for people with disabilities. 
 MassAbility is more than just a new name. It is an opportunity to demonstrate our 
commitment to tearing down systemic barriers for people with disabilities in the workforce and 
in our communities. Changing the words we use when we speak about disability is one more step 
toward building more inclusive and equitable communities that amplify the inherent worth and 
potential of all their members. 
 For all these reasons, I urge your favorable consideration of this legislation. 
 Respectfully submitted, 
 
 Maura T. Healey, 
 Governor

An Act to rename the Massachusetts Rehabilitation Commission, Massability, to reflect the self-
determination and limitless possibilities of people with disabilities. 
 
 Whereas, The deferred operation of this act would tend to defeat its purpose, which is to 
promote equity and increase opportunities for persons with disabilities by renaming the 
commission and updating language used to more accurately reflect the values and core mission 
of the agency, therefore it is hereby declared to be an emergency law, necessary for the 
immediate preservation of the public convenience.
