## H60 — Massachusetts Information Privacy and Security Act (MIPSA); new G.L. c.93M (comprehensive controller/processor privacy regime)

Definitions (c.93M s.2), scope/thresholds (s.3: $25M revenue / data broker / 100,000 individuals for ss.7-17, 26), conflicts (s.4), de-identified info (s.23), limitations (s.24), severability (s.29) attach to the substantive propositions below.

1. mipsa-processing-principles — Duty that personal information be processed fairly, purpose-limited, minimized, accurate, storage-limited, and secure. c.93M s.5. "adequate, relevant and limited to what is reasonably necessary in relation to the purposes" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA data-minimization]
2. mipsa-lawful-basis — Processing permitted only on an enumerated lawful basis (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, weighed legitimate interest), with legitimate-interest basis barred for profiling producing legal effects. c.93M s.6. "Processing shall be lawful and in compliance with this chapter only if"
3. mipsa-privacy-notice — Mandatory point-of-collection privacy notice (categories, purposes, sales, retention, foreign-adversary access), plus large-data-holder 10-year notice archive. c.93M s.7. "a reasonably accessible, clear and meaningful privacy notice" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA transparency duty]
4. mipsa-optout-sale-ads — Right to opt out of sale, targeted cross-contextual advertising, and targeted first-party advertising, honored within 15 days, via authorized agent and a universal opt-out preference signal with AG technical specs. c.93M s.8; links s.17(b)-(d); signal specs s.25(u)(3). "the right to opt out of the processing of the individual's personal information" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA/state-comprehensive opt-out + universal opt-out signal]
5. mipsa-sensitive-info-consent — Opt-in consent required before processing or selling sensitive information (geolocation, biometric/genetic, children's, health, orientation, immigration status, etc.), with 12-month re-request cooldown. c.93M s.9. "shall not otherwise process an individual's sensitive information without first obtaining the consent" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA sensitive-data rules]
6. mipsa-right-access-portability — Right to obtain specific pieces of processed personal information in a portable, transmittable format. c.93M s.10. "the specific pieces of personal information that the controller has processed about the individual" [FLAG: MDPPA consumer-rights overlap]
7. mipsa-right-delete — Right to deletion with downstream processor/third-party notification and enumerated retention exceptions. c.93M s.11. "the right to request that a controller delete any personal information processed" [FLAG: MDPPA consumer-rights overlap]
8. mipsa-right-correct — Right to correction of inaccurate personal information. c.93M s.12. "correct inaccurate personal information processed about the individual" [FLAG: MDPPA consumer-rights overlap]
9. mipsa-right-revoke-consent — Right to revoke previously given consent via an easy, conspicuous mechanism. c.93M s.13. "the right to revoke consent that the individual previously gave to a controller"
   (Request procedures ss.14-15, 17 — verifiable requests, 45-day response, two designated methods, homepage opt-out links — attach to props 4-9.)
10. mipsa-nondiscrimination — Prohibition on denying, degrading, or repricing goods/services for exercising rights, with bona fide loyalty-program exception; contract waivers void. c.93M ss.16, 18. "shall not discriminate against an individual for exercising in good faith any of the rights" [FLAG: MDPPA anti-retaliation overlap]
11. mipsa-processor-contracts — Mandatory controller-processor contracts (instructions, confidentiality, sub-processor objection, audits, deletion at end of services); due-diligence duty. c.93M s.19. "A contract between a controller and a processor shall govern the processor's procedures"
12. mipsa-databroker-registration — Annual data broker registration with the AG ($200 fee, public registry web page, centralized broker opt-out/delete mechanism). c.93M s.20, s.25(p)(2)-(3); nonregistration penalties s.25(f) ($500/day, cap $100,000/yr). "the controller shall register with the attorney general" [FLAG: resembles standalone data-broker-registry bills]
13. mipsa-risk-assessments — Pre-processing documented risk assessments for sale/targeted advertising, high-risk profiling, and sensitive information, plus algorithmic-system detail for large data holders; disclosable to AG by CID, exempt from public records. c.93M s.21. "carry out and document a risk assessment of the impact" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA data-protection-assessment duty]
14. mipsa-antidiscrimination-processing — Prohibition on processing personal information in a manner that discriminates in the equal enjoyment of goods or services by protected class. c.93M s.22. "discriminates in, or otherwise makes unavailable, the equal enjoyment of goods or services" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA civil-rights provision]
15. mipsa-ag-enforcement — Exclusive AG enforcement: CIDs, notice and 30-day cure (with carve-outs for large holders/brokers), civil penalties to $7,500/violation, penalty factors, annual reports, complaint mechanism, templates, opt-out-signal and other regulations, biennial inflation indexing. c.93M s.25. "civil penalties of up to 7,500 dollars for each violation" [FLAG: MDPPA enforcement overlap; cure-period design is a classic companion-difference point]
16. mipsa-breach-private-action — Private right of action limited to security breaches caused by failure of reasonable cybersecurity controls ($500/individual/incident or actual damages), with punitive-damages safe harbor for NIST/ISO/CIS/PCI-conforming written programs. c.93M s.26. "damages from the controller in an amount up to 500 dollars per individual per incident" [FLAG: contrast with MDPPA private right of action — different scope]
17. mipsa-privacy-fund — Dedicated Massachusetts Privacy Fund receiving penalties and fees to support AG privacy work. c.93M s.27. "a separate special fund to be known as the Massachusetts Privacy Fund"
18. mipsa-reciprocity — Deemed compliance for entities complying with another jurisdiction's law the AG certifies as equally or more protective. c.93M s.28. "equally or more protective of personal information than the provisions of this chapter"
19. mipsa-nonprofit-highered-scope — Extension of the chapter to nonprofits and institutions of higher education (delayed 30-month effective date). c.93M s.30; SECTION 2. "shall apply to nonprofit organizations and institutions of higher education"

## S227 — Senate companion to H60; text verbatim identical

Propositions 1-19 identical to H60, same section numbers, no textual differences (whitespace only). All bill-proposition edges duplicate H60's with no stricter/weaker deltas.

## S1896 — Accountability in the sale of personal data; new G.L. c.65D (taxation of sales of data) + c.29 s.2RRRRR

1. data-seller-dor-registration — Annual registration with the Department of Revenue by any entity selling/exchanging Massachusetts residents' personal information for consideration, disclosing data types, buyers, sources, and attributable gross income; $100/day delinquency fine; DOR must report a recommended tax design to the legislature within 12 months. c.65D ss.1-2 (defs attach), s.6 (social media deemed in-state). "must register with the department of revenue" [FLAG: related to H60/S227 data-broker registration (prop 12) — same target (data sellers), different mechanism/agency (DOR tax-base disclosure vs AG privacy registry); align with caution]
2. ice-sale-prohibition — Ban on selling or exchanging personal information for consideration to ICE or to any intermediary that plans to. c.65D s.5. "shall sell or exchange personal information for consideration to the United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement"
3. municipal-broadband-fund — [OUT-OF-DOMAIN, fiscal/broadband] Municipal Broadband Fund credited with the contemplated data-sale tax revenue, administered by DTC. c.65D s.4; c.29 s.2RRRRR (SECTION 2). "a fund to be known as the Municipal Broadband Fund"
   (s.3 severability attaches.)

## H1428 — Relative to personal information (police officers)

1. police-officer-pii-posting-ban — Prohibition, with criminal penalty (fine to $5,000 and/or 2.5 years), on publicly posting a police officer's or their spouse's/child's home address, phone, email, photos, or vehicle information without consent. New G.L. c.41 s.98H (SECTION 1). "shall publicly post or publicly display on the internet or make publicly available in any manner the personal information" [FLAG: anti-doxxing/disclosure-restriction mechanism; aligns with government-employee address-shield bills, not the comprehensive-regime family]

## S218 — Ensuring internet security and privacy

1. isp-optin-consent — ISPs/telecoms with state or municipal franchise/right-of-way agreements (or using such facilities) may not collect, use, disclose, or disseminate customer personal information from service use without express written approval; no surcharge on and no denial of service to non-consenting customers. New G.L. c.93 s.115. "without express written approval from the customer" [FLAG: opt-in consent mechanism, distinct from MIPSA/MDPPA opt-out mechanism; ISP-specific target]

## H1555 — Internet bill of rights; new G.L. c.93M (near-verbatim GDPR transposition, ss.1-65)

Definitions (s.1), scope (s.2), sectoral derogations (ss.58-64), transition (SECTION 2), evaluation reports (s.65) attach.

1. ibor-processing-principles — GDPR art.5-style principles: lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, security, controller accountability. c.93M s.3. "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes" [FLAG: same mechanism as H60/S227 prop 1 (both GDPR-art.5 derived); H1555 has no size thresholds — stricter coverage, same proposition candidate; resembles MDPPA data-minimization]
2. ibor-lawful-basis — Processing lawful only on six enumerated bases (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests), with compatibility test for secondary purposes. c.93M s.4. "Processing shall be legal only if and to the extent that at least 1 of the following applies" [FLAG: same proposition as H60/S227 prop 2; H1555 adds public-task basis]
3. ibor-consent-conditions-child-16 — Consent must be demonstrable, distinguishable, withdrawable as easily as given; information-society services to children under 16 require parental consent. c.93M s.5. "Where the child is below the age of 16 years, such processing shall be lawful only if" [FLAG: child-consent age mechanism absent from H60 (which uses COPPA under-13 via definitions)]
4. ibor-special-categories-ban — Default prohibition on processing racial/ethnic, political, religious, union, genetic, biometric, health, and sex-life data, subject to enumerated exceptions (explicit consent, employment, vital interest, public health, research). c.93M s.6. "Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs" [FLAG: same target as H60/S227 prop 5; stricter mechanism (prohibition-with-exceptions vs consent-gate) — treat as same proposition, note strictness]
5. ibor-transparency-notice — Mandatory information to data subjects at collection and (within 1 month) when data is obtained from other sources; 1-month response deadline for rights requests. c.93M ss.7-9. "the controller shall, at the time when personal data is obtained, provide the data subject with all of the following" [FLAG: same proposition as H60/S227 prop 3]
6. ibor-right-access — Right to confirmation of processing and access to the data, recipients, retention, source, and automated-decision logic, with free copy. c.93M s.10. "confirmation as to whether or not personal data concerning the data subject is being processed" [FLAG: same proposition as H60/S227 prop 6 (access half)]
7. ibor-right-rectification — Right to rectification and completion of inaccurate personal data. c.93M s.11. "the rectification of inaccurate personal data concerning the data subject" [FLAG: same as H60/S227 prop 8]
8. ibor-right-erasure — Right to erasure including duty to inform downstream controllers of erasure requests (right to be forgotten), with freedom-of-expression/research exceptions. c.93M s.12; recipient-communication duty s.14. "the erasure of personal data concerning the data subject without undue delay" [FLAG: same as H60/S227 prop 7; stricter (public-data delinking)]
9. ibor-right-restriction — Right to obtain restriction of processing (data marked and frozen pending accuracy or objection disputes). c.93M s.13. "the right to obtain from the controller restriction of processing" [FLAG: no H60/S227 or MDPPA counterpart — distinct proposition]
10. ibor-right-portability — Right to receive provided data in machine-readable format and transmit controller-to-controller. c.93M s.15. "in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format" [FLAG: same as portability half of H60/S227 prop 6]
11. ibor-right-object — Right to object to legitimate-interest/public-task processing, and absolute right to object to direct-marketing processing including profiling. c.93M s.16. "the right to object at any time to processing of personal data concerning the data subject for the marketing" [FLAG: direct-marketing component functionally parallels H60/S227 opt-out (prop 4) but is a different mechanism (objection right vs opt-out-of-sale/ads + signal); recommend separate IDs with noted kinship]
12. ibor-automated-decisions — Right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with legal or similar effects, with human-intervention safeguard. c.93M s.17. "right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling" [FLAG: H60/S227 only restrict the lawful basis for such profiling (s.6(b)) — different mechanism, related target]
13. ibor-design-default-records — Data protection by design and by default plus records-of-processing duty (250-employee small-entity carve-out) and staff-instruction limits. c.93M ss.18-19, 23-24. "measures, such as pseudonymization, which are designed to implement data-protection principles, such as data minimization"
14. ibor-representative — Out-of-state controllers/processors targeting Massachusetts must designate an in-commonwealth representative. c.93M s.21. "shall designate in writing a representative in the commonwealth"
15. ibor-processor-contracts — Mandatory binding contracts governing processors and sub-processors (documented instructions, confidentiality, audit, deletion/return). c.93M ss.20, 22. "Processing by a processor shall be governed by a contract or other legal act" [FLAG: same proposition as H60/S227 prop 11]
16. ibor-security — Risk-appropriate technical and organizational security measures including pseudonymization and encryption. c.93M s.26. "a level of security appropriate to the risk" [FLAG: overlaps existing c.93H/201 CMR 17 regime]
17. ibor-breach-notification — Breach notification to the AG within 72 hours and to affected data subjects when high-risk, with documentation duty. c.93M ss.27-28. "not later than 72 hours after having become aware of it, notify the personal data breach to the attorney general" [FLAG: duplicates/tightens existing c.93H breach notification — align with breach-notification family, not present in H60/S227]
18. ibor-dpia-prior-consultation — Data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing plus mandatory prior consultation with the AG when residual risk is high. c.93M ss.29-30. "an assessment of the impact of the envisaged processing operations on the protection of personal data" [FLAG: same proposition family as H60/S227 prop 13; prior-consultation element is stricter and unique]
19. ibor-data-protection-officer — Mandatory data protection officer for public bodies and large-scale monitors/special-category processors, with independence guarantees. c.93M ss.31-33. "The controller and the processor shall designate a data protection officer" [FLAG: no counterpart in any other bill here]
20. ibor-codes-certification — AG-approved codes of conduct, accredited monitoring bodies, and voluntary certification/seals ("Commonwealth Data Protection Seal"). c.93M ss.34-37. "data protection certification mechanisms and of data protection seals and marks"
21. ibor-cross-border-transfers — Transfers of personal data outside the commonwealth permitted only under AG adequacy decisions, appropriate safeguards (standard clauses, binding corporate rules), or derogations; foreign court orders enforceable only via MLAT-type agreements. c.93M ss.38-44. "where the attorney general has decided that the foreign destination in question ensures an adequate level of protection" [FLAG: unique — closest H60/S227 analog is the notice item on China/Russia/Iran/North Korea access (H60 s.7(a)(11)), a much weaker mechanism]
22. ibor-ag-supervision-fines — AG as GDPR-style supervisory authority: audits, orders, processing bans, complaint handling, urgency procedures, annual reports, and administrative fines to $10M/2% or $20M/4% of worldwide turnover. c.93M ss.45-50, 51-52, 56-57. "administrative fines up to $20,000,000, or in the case of an undertaking, up to 4 per cent of the total worldwide annual turnover" [FLAG: same role as H60/S227 prop 15 but different mechanism (administrative fines vs 93A-style civil action with cure period) — probably separate propositions]
23. ibor-private-remedy-compensation — Judicial remedy against controllers/processors, compensation for material and non-material damage with joint-and-several liability, and nonprofit representative actions. c.93M ss.53-55; s.54. "Any person who has suffered material or non-material damage as a result of an infringement" [FLAG: full private right of action — much broader than H60/S227 breach-only PRA (prop 16); companion-difference-grade distinction]

## H3179 — Internet service provider data

1. isp-optin-consent — Identical first paragraph to S218: written-approval opt-in for ISP customer data, no surcharge, no service denial. New G.L. c.93 s.115. "without express written approval from the customer" [SAME proposition as S218 prop 1; paragraph is verbatim identical]
2. isp-sale-written-authorization — ISP prohibited from selling customer personally identifiable information absent express written authorization, revocable in writing at any time. c.93 s.115 (second paragraph, H3179 only). "prohibit the sale of a customer's personally identifiable information unless the customer expressly, in writing, authorizes" [FLAG: this is exactly the point on which the companions differ — separate proposition per grain rule]

## H3831 — Municipal/Commonwealth public internet service; new G.L. c.7D ss.12-14

OUT-OF-DOMAIN portions: s.12 (municipal broadband build-out solicitation, 50% cost share, creation of "Commonwealth ISP"), s.13(a)-(d),(f) (anti-censorship, public policies, net neutrality, contractor neutrality, oversight process), s.14(1)(i)-(k) and 14(2) (partner-policy review, ombudsman, public complaint process, contractor penalties — governance scaffolding attaching to the duties below).

In-domain propositions (target: municipally/state-owned broadband service, not private controllers):

1. muni-isp-data-minimization — Public broadband may not collect, use, disclose, or retain internet-usage data beyond service necessity, must de-identify and drop data when no longer needed. c.7D s.14(1)(a)-(b). "not collect, use, disclose or retain internet data...beyond what is necessary to provide, maintain and secure" [FLAG: resembles MDPPA data-minimization and H60/S227 prop 1, but public-utility target]
2. muni-isp-collection-notice — Public, accessible, translated notice of what data is collected/retained/shared and retention periods. c.7D s.14(1)(c). "provide public notice, made available in translations and in forms that users with disabilities can access"
3. muni-isp-thirdparty-optin — No sharing of user data with third parties except necessary contractors or with users' opt-in consent. c.7D s.14(1)(d). "not share data information with 3rd parties...or with opt-in consent of users" [FLAG: parallels S218/H3179 opt-in mechanism, different (public) target]
4. muni-isp-anonymous-wifi — No requiring users to identify themselves to access public wifi. c.7D s.14(1)(e). "not require users to identify themselves in order to gain access to public wifi services"
5. muni-isp-data-profile-access — Any user may request their data profile and receive a report of information collected, stored, or shared. c.7D s.13(e). "request their data profile and receive a report of any information that has been collected, stored or shared" [FLAG: access-right kin of H60/S227 prop 6 / H1555 prop 6]
6. muni-isp-security-breach-notice — Reasonable security and industry-par encryption plus prompt user notification of any data breach. c.7D s.14(1)(f)-(h). "promptly notify users in case of any breach of data information" [FLAG: breach-notification family, c.93H kin]
7. muni-isp-warrant-requirement — User data released to law enforcement only with a warrant, and the user must be notified of any government request and the requester's identity. c.7D s.14(3). "shall not be provided to law enforcement without a warrant" [FLAG: government-acquisition-of-stored-data mechanism; kin to location-shield/warrant bills (H1653/S27 family), not MDPPA]

## CROSS-BILL

- H60 = S227: verbatim identical companions; all 19 propositions shared 1:1, zero textual deltas.
- S218 = H3179 paragraph 1: verbatim identical (isp-optin-consent). H3179 adds exactly one proposition (isp-sale-written-authorization) — the only companion-pair difference in this set besides none in H60/S227.
- S1896 vs H60/S227: data-seller registration overlaps data-broker registration (H60 prop 12) in target but not mechanism (DOR tax-base filing + return vs AG privacy registry + public opt-out page). Recommend distinct proposition IDs with a recorded kinship edge. S1896's ICE-sale ban has no counterpart anywhere in the eight; it is a sale-restriction mechanism (compare MIPSA sale opt-out only at the individual's election — different mechanism).
- H1428 replicates nothing from H60/S227/H1555; it belongs with the public-records/disclosure-restriction subdomain (police-officer PII), not the comprehensive family.
- H1555 vs H60/S227: nine mechanism-level matches (candidate shared IDs, H1555 generally stricter): processing principles, lawful bases, privacy notice, access, portability, deletion/erasure, correction/rectification, sensitive-data gate, processor contracts, risk assessment/DPIA. H1555-only mechanisms: restriction of processing, DPO, in-state representative, 72-hour breach notification, cross-border adequacy regime, codes/certification, administrative fines, general damages PRA, automated-decision right, under-16 parental consent. H60/S227-only mechanisms: opt-out of sale/targeted ads + universal opt-out signal, non-discrimination/financial incentives, data broker registration, reciprocity, privacy fund, breach-only PRA with NIST safe harbor, nonprofit/higher-ed staged scope. Enforcement architectures differ in mechanism (AG civil action + cure vs GDPR administrative fines) — keep separate propositions; likewise opt-out (H60 s.8) vs objection/direct-marketing (H1555 s.16) — different mechanism, note kinship.
- ISP opt-in cluster: S218/H3179 (private franchised ISPs) and H3831 s.14(1)(d) (public ISP) share the opt-in-before-collection/sharing mechanism at different targets; different propositions, kinship edge worth recording.
- MDPPA (H83/S25) alignment flags, for cross-family ID mapping: data minimization (H60/S227 s.5; H1555 s.3; H3831 s.14(1)(a)); opt-out of sale/targeted advertising + opt-out preference signal (H60/S227 s.8); sensitive-data consent (H60/S227 s.9; H1555 s.6); consumer rights access/delete/correct/portability (H60/S227 ss.10-13; H1555 ss.10-15; H3831 s.13(e)); non-discrimination (H60/S227 s.16); risk/data-protection assessments (H60/S227 s.21; H1555 s.29); civil-rights processing ban (H60/S227 s.22); AG enforcement with cure period (H60/S227 s.25); private right of action scope (H60/S227 s.26 breach-only vs H1555 ss.53-55 general — the axis on which the DPPA family also varies).
