## Verdict

At head 2b02165 the pilot substantially satisfies BRIEF.md's four goals and its answer to Goal 5 survives every defect found: 16 of 289 propositions reached the statute book, 5.5%, and none of the confirmed fixes moves that number outside rounding. Goals 1 through 3 are executed well past the standard of the brief, and the enacted-side universe is genuinely complete. Six independent auditors, each put through an adversarial verifier, could not break the census, the link graph, the identity-basis labels, the enacted-chapter extraction, or the neutrality rules.

The DEFINITION OF DONE does not hold universally. "A skeptical stranger can pick any proposition, click its citations, and confirm its entire story" fails for roughly 24 of 289 propositions (about 8%) and for 31 of the 80 analytic-identity rows in the verification queue. In each case the stranger clicks the cited official page and finds it contradicting, not confirming, the ledger. Two headline sentences in the two-page memo are false against the project's own generated tables. None of these is a fabrication of measurement; they are parser gaps, stale prose, and evidence-presentation failures. All are cheap to fix.

Findings 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 below I reproduced myself with executed code; the rest I accept on the verifiers' reproductions and note where I did not re-run them.

**2. P1 - The verification queue's "verbatim side-by-side excerpts" are analyst prose on both sides for 23 of 80 rows, and one excerpt asserts a legal element the bill does not contain.**

`memo/codebook.md:62`, `codebook.md:56`, `findings.md:15` and `findings.md:53` all promise verbatim excerpts from official texts; `atoms.py:783` labels the table "Verbatim quotes (from the cached official texts)". I wrote my own generous matcher (case and punctuation insensitive, splits on " ... ", drops standalone digit tokens so PDF line numbers cannot cause false negatives, strips trailing "(2024 c.206 s.15)" style cites) and got 58 of 155 `QUOTES` entries non-verbatim, and in `data/verification_queue.csv` 31 of 80 `proposition_identity` rows with at least one non-verbatim side, 23 with both. Those 23 show the analyst's own summary next to a link to the bill, which is precisely the artifact the brief's evidence rule was written to prevent.

Most of the 58 are honest analyst descriptions with pinpoint section cites, mislabeled rather than wrong. One is not. `atoms.py:1043` gives S1116 the excerpt "a person may bring a civil action ... doxing ... disclosure of personally identifying information with intent to harass", published as `excerpt_a` of `verification_queue.csv:83`. I confirmed against the cached text: S1116 contains no "harass" under any synonym, no "civil action", and no "personally identifying". Its actual operative clause is "may pursue a cause of action for doxing" and its intent element is intent to cause "stalking, physical harm to person, or serious property damage". "Intent to harass" is the comparator H1707's element. The queue row asks the reviewer to judge whether S1116 states the same smallest change as H1707, and the excerpt manufactures agreement on the exact axis where the two statutes differ. This is the same failure class as the 25-entry fanned-quote block removed in the previous pass, and it violates the rule the project wrote at `atoms.py:1265` in response to it: "A quote attributed to a bill must come from THAT bill's text."

`09_checks.py:52-55` asserts only that excerpts are non-empty and contain no "quote missing" placeholder. Nothing tests that an excerpt occurs in the document it is attributed to.

Fix: add a verbatim assertion to `09_checks.py` over both `QUOTES` and the queue excerpts, using exactly the tolerances above; then either replace the ~40 legitimate summaries with real excerpts or move them into a separately labeled column so the word "verbatim" is true of what remains. Replace the S1116 entry with the bill's actual cause-of-action and intent language, and let the difference show, since that is what the reviewer must weigh.

Related and folded in here: `atoms.py:882` attributes H1690's "an eviction record sealed under this section" to H4356, which says "a court record"; `atoms.py:885/:887` assert "separate collection categories" for H3003 and H4040, a phrase in neither bill nor in 2023 c.28 s.7 (both say "separate collection and tabulations"), and both of those are published in queue rows 96 and 97. Two of the 40 kinship excerpts (P-266 and P-274) are likewise paraphrase in a field documented as verbatim.

**3. P1 - P-295 and P-296 are labeled `enacted_other_vehicle` on a premise the project's own link graph contradicts, and the substitute evidence the detail cites does not exist for three of the six carriers it names.**

`data/proposition_fates.csv` states for both: "filed carriers H1690,H4138,H4356,S2834,S2850,S956 have no official chain to it (absorption established by text adjudication, see data/enacted_adjudication.csv and the absorbed_into_vehicle links)". **4. P2 - Two of the sixteen enacted propositions describe mechanisms that the enacting chapter does not contain.**

Same reader-facing failure, two instances, both verified by me directly.

P-332 is defined as two severable mechanisms: "Camera systems may capture images only upon violation; destruction within 48 hours of final disposition (variant: 30-day/1-year schedules)". The capture limit is present in all six c.90J-line carriers (H3375, H3393, S1483, S2275, H4166, H4287) and absent from all six c.90 s.14C-line carriers (H3306, H3336, H3440, H4450, H4940, S3005) and from 2024 c.399, which has zero hits for "only be captured", "only take photographs" or "48 hours". Because the destruction half was enacted, the whole proposition is `enacted_as_filed` via H4940/c.399. The project splits exactly this collection/retention pair everywhere else (P-281 vs P-282 in H4844; P-193 vs P-194 in the ALPR family), and `findings.md:24` already narrates c.399 as enacting only "the 30-day/1-year destruction regime with annual attestation", so the prose knows what the dataset denies. Under the pipeline's own branch order the split capture-limit proposition would come out `sent_to_study` with `dropped_in_consolidation = yes`, which is the memo's central subject converted into an enactment.

P-301 is described as "Bus-camera photographs may not be used to identify vehicle occupants or contents; mandatory redaction before notices issue" and lists two enacted vehicles, c.363 and c.399. "redact" and "obscure" appear in only 3 of its 12 carriers (S2884, H5154, S2600, the c.363 line). `chaptertext.py 2024 399` has zero redaction hits; what it has is the opposite savings clause, "no notice of liability issued under this section shall be dismissed solely because a photograph ... allow for the identification of the operator". The distinction does exist in `atoms.py` QUOTES, but `07_links.py:380` emits QUOTES only for inferred-analytic edges and every P-301 edge is verified, so it reaches no deliverable.

Fix: split P-332 per the P-281/P-282 precedent, retiring it with a RETIRED entry; for P-301 either split the redaction duty or narrow the description to the shared rule and record the variant on the affected edges.

**6. P2 - The memo and codebook say the census histories contain exactly one adverse roll call; the pipeline's own table records two. Separately, the fate category `rejected_by_recorded_vote` cannot be emitted by any code path.**

Four auditors found the first half independently; I reproduced it. Scanning all 142 census histories with the pipeline's own two regexes gives 73 recorded tallies, of which exactly two have nays exceeding yeas: S2834 Amendment 25 (6-34) and H4040 "Motion to suspend Rule 40 rejected - 25 YEAS to 132 NAYS (See YEA and NAY No. 31)". The H4040 action is unstricken, sits in the cached official API response, and is published in `data/bill_fates.csv` and propagated into the `roll_calls` column of P-297 and P-298. So the memo's cited example attaches to no proposition, while the one it omits attaches to two. Nothing in the repo defines "adverse roll call" in a way that would exclude a rejected procedural motion.

The second half compounds it. `grep rejected_by_recorded_vote` returns two hits repo-wide, both prose: the `08_fates.py` docstring and `codebook.md:29`. `bill_status` collects roll-call strings and never compares yeas to nays; the fate ladder has five reachable outcomes. `codebook.md:13` says recorded-vote rejection "is parsed for but did not occur"; it is not parsed for. An injected 12-145 floor defeat is classified `died_no_recorded_action` with the detail "ended with no further action; no public explanation" and the suite exits 0. BRIEF.md Goal 4 lists rejection by recorded vote as a required category.

I checked the substance and the conclusion survives: no bill-level rejection language occurs anywhere in the corpus, so "no proposition was rejected on the record" is independently true, not true by construction. What is wrong is the supporting count and the claim that the code looks.

Fix: state two adverse roll calls, neither on a policy matter in the domain, and define the term in the codebook. Either implement the adversity branch or delete it from the docstring and rewrite `codebook.md:13`. Add an assertion over the `roll_calls` columns so the sentence is machine-checked like the rest. Optionally add the `(See YEA and NAY in Supplement, No. N)` House format, which drops 24 favorable veto-override votes whose Senate twins on the same items are captured.

**7. P2 - At least one whole-bill in-domain filing is accounted for nowhere: H3524.**

`grep '^H3524,'` returns zero rows in `census.csv`, `corpus_scan.csv` and `corpus_triage_verdicts.csv`. Its complete text is 840 characters and is nothing but two personal-information disclosure restrictions: section 1 bars a municipality from publishing "the name, or other individually identifying information, of a veteran still owing a tax", and section 2 amends c.62C s.21 so "the name and address of a veteran shall not be published as part of said list". That is verbatim the codebook's included subdomain "restrictions on disclosure of personal information held in government records", the same mechanism as S194 (lottery-winner identity, included, P-254). It cannot be a program-incident exclusion because the nondisclosure rule is the bill's only content.

The cause is narrow term-form specificity, not the active/passive distinction the original report proposed: "shall not be published" is passive, the construction `record_nondisclosure` already screens for, but the alternation lists only "disclosed|made public"; and `personal(?:ly)?(?: identif(?:iable|ying))? information` does not cover "individually identifying". The codebook does disclose term-form dependence as its residual recall limitation, and the auditors' sweep of 14 other unscanned nondisclosure filings found them all correctly out under existing rules, so this is one filing rather than a class. But prior passes treated structurally identical misses as defects to fix (the fifth pass admitted H1939 and others, the sixth admitted S1136 and S1503), so consistency requires fixing this one too. Impact if fixed: 142 in-domain filings becomes 143, at least one new proposition, and every headline regenerates.

Fix: add "published|released" to the passive alternation and "individually identif(ying|iable) information" to `personal_information`, rerun 01b and 04, adjudicate whatever surfaces, and atomize H3524.

**8. P2 - P-335's fate detail states a terminal record the cited page does not contain, and a consolidation drop is recorded as no drop.**

The enacted universe is complete and independently corroborated. The official year indexes resolve to exactly 2023 Acts 1-89, 2024 Acts 1-407 and 2024 Resolves 1, with no pagination or letter-suffix chapters; approval dates parsed from every chapter body are monotonic in chapter order with one adjacent inversion, leaving no room for a missing chapter; and the 2023 Resolves 404 is confirmed three independent ways. ## On the definition of done

It holds for the large majority of propositions and fails for a specific, enumerable minority: the 19 at `referred` stage whose bill pages show a hearing, P-295 and P-296 whose detail denies a chain the project itself publishes, P-332 and P-301 whose descriptions claim mechanisms absent from the chapters they cite, and P-335 whose detail contradicts the last line of its own history page. Call it 24 of 289. Add the 31 of 80 queue rows where the reviewer is shown the analyst's summary and told to check the bill. Every one of these is a presentation or parser defect over a measurement that is itself correct, and every fix is under a day's work. The domain-level answer to Goal 5 is not in question.
